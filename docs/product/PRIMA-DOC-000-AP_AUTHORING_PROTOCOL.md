# PRIMA-DOC-000-AP  
# Authoring Protocol for the Founding Charter of PRIMA

**Mandatory Governance Protocol**

---

## 1. Purpose of this Protocol

This Protocol does not replace the Founding Charter.

It establishes **how** the Founding Charter and all documents that claim authority under it must be written, tested, and kept coherent for decades.

Its purpose is to ensure that PRIMA’s governance remains:

- educationally grounded,  
- ethically defensible,  
- teacher-sovereign,  
- learner-dignifying,  
- practically enforceable,  
- and free of transient technical fashion.

**If a future document conflicts with the Constitution (PRIMA-DOC-000), the Constitution prevails.**  
**If a drafting process conflicts with this Protocol, this Protocol prevails.**

---

## 2. Role of the Drafting Commission

Constitutional text shall be drafted as if by a commission competent in:

Education · Learning Sciences · Curriculum Design · Educational Psychology · Inclusive Education · Human–Computer Interaction · Artificial Intelligence (as educational assistance) · Software Architecture · Product Strategy · Digital Ethics · Governance.

The Commission drafts **constitution**, not marketing, not brand copy, not transient architecture notes.

---

## 3. Constitutional Requirements (every article)

Every article of the Founding Charter — and every normative article in subordinate governing documents — must satisfy **all** of the following.

### 3.1 Timeless
The article must remain valid regardless of future tools.  
**Forbidden:** named programming languages, frameworks, model vendors, or ephemeral implementation recipes.

### 3.2 Educational First
The article must reinforce educational value. Technology exists only to serve pedagogy.

### 3.3 Teacher Sovereignty
Teacher judgment is superior to automation. Assistance may support; the teacher decides.

### 3.4 Learner Dignity
No article may reduce learners to labels. Diversity of learning is a strength.

### 3.5 Ethical Consistency
The article must be ethically defensible. Under uncertainty, prefer the option that best protects teachers and learners.

### 3.6 Practical Governance
The article must be enforceable. Avoid vague aspiration. Write actionable principle.

---

## 4. Constitutional Tests (mandatory pass)

Before an article is adopted, it must answer **YES** to each test:

| Test | Question |
|------|----------|
| **Timelessness** | Would this article still be valid in twenty years? |
| **Pedagogical** | Does this article improve education? |
| **Teacher** | Does this article increase or protect teacher autonomy? |
| **Ethics** | Can this article be defended ethically? |
| **Engineering** | Can software teams implement this principle in practice? |
| **Governance** | Can future product decisions be evaluated against this article? |

A single **NO** returns the article to drafting.

---

## 5. Writing Style

Formal. Precise. Authoritative. Minimal. Timeless. Professional.  
Inspirational without sentimentality.  

Avoid slogans, buzzwords, marketing language, and needless repetition.

---

## 6. Required Document Hierarchy

Constitutional documents shall use:

**PREAMBLE** → **CHAPTERS** → **ARTICLES** → **CLAUSES** (as needed) → **IMPLEMENTATION PRINCIPLES** → **QUALITY GATES** → **FINAL DECLARATION**

### 6.1 Mandatory article anatomy

Every Article shall contain, in order:

1. **Purpose** — why the article exists  
2. **Principle** — the binding norm  
3. **Practical implication** — what stewards must do differently  
4. **Enforcement rule** — what is forbidden or required at release  

Articles that lack any of the four elements are incomplete.

## 8. The Seven Immutable Principles (authorship rule)

The Charter must define seven principles that:

- may never be violated,  
- override every future operational decision,  
- and bind every feature before release.

Drafting of these principles must pass all Constitutional Tests in §4 with heightened scrutiny. Weakening them is not an ordinary amendment (see Charter Amendment Process).

---

## 9. Constitutional Compliance Review (mandatory)

Every proposed feature, material capability, or governing document change must answer:

1. Does it respect pedagogy?  
2. Does it respect teachers?  
3. Does it respect learners?  
4. Does it improve accessibility?  
5. Does it preserve transparency?  
6. Does it respect privacy?  
7. Does it remain consistent with the Constitution?

**If any answer is NO, the proposal returns to the design phase.** Release is forbidden.

Review records shall state which Charter articles were examined.

---

## 10. Engineering Quality Gates (authorship of gates)

The Charter must define mandatory gates, including at least:

Pedagogical · Experience (UX) · Accessibility · AI · Privacy · Security · Performance · Engineering · Governance  

**Release is impossible until every gate passes.**

Gate definitions must be actionable by review teams without naming transient tools.

---

## 11. Amendment Discipline (for this Protocol)

Amendments to this Authoring Protocol require Founding Council (or successor constitutional steward) approval, must preserve Teacher Sovereignty and Learner Dignity, and must not create loopholes that allow Charter evasion.
