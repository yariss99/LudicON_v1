# PRIMA-DOC-002  
# Product Manifest

**The Product Design Philosophy of PRIMA**

Adopted as the permanent handbook for conceiving, designing, evaluating, and improving every feature, workflow, interface, and assisting capability in the PRIMA ecosystem.

---

# Preamble

The Founding Charter defines what must never be violated.  
The North Star defines the future we build toward.  
This Product Manifest defines **what makes a feature worthy of PRIMA**.

It does not describe functionality.  
It defines the philosophy of building.

**Whenever implementation speed conflicts with educational quality, educational quality prevails.**

Core question for every section:

> **What makes a feature truly belong inside PRIMA?**

---

# PART I — The Philosophy of Product Design

### 1.1 Product as pedagogical instrument
PRIMA is built as an instrument of teaching craft. A feature belongs only when it strengthens the teacher’s ability to design worthy learning and the learner’s ability to reach it with dignity.

### 1.2 Pedagogy leads; technology follows
Pedagogy defines purpose. Technology supplies means. Experience makes means usable. Assisting intelligence proposes options. Human decision commits. Educational impact judges worth.

### 1.3 The relationship of forces

| Force | Role in product design |
|-------|-------------------------|
| **Pedagogy** | Sets the aim and the constraints of integrity |
| **Technology** | Extends reach; never redefines the aim |
| **User experience** | Reduces friction so craft remains possible under time pressure |
| **Assisting intelligence** | Advises and drafts; never authorizes |
| **Human decision making** | Remains the seat of pedagogical responsibility |
| **Educational impact** | Is the final measure of whether a feature deserved to exist |

### 1.4 Worthiness before velocity
A fast feature that harms clarity, autonomy, access, or dignity is not progress. Slowing to protect educational quality is loyalty to mission.

### 1.5 Dual value as product law
Every major capability must reduce unnecessary teacher workload **and** improve learning opportunity. One without the other is incomplete. Neither without dignity is acceptable.

# PART II — The Definition of a PRIMA Feature

A high-quality PRIMA feature is a discrete capability that a teacher can understand, control, and complete as professional craft.

### 2.1 A feature deserves to exist only if it
1. Serves a real educational problem teachers face.  
2. Makes teacher work clearer, lighter, or more controllable.  
3. Improves learning access, coherence, fairness, or depth—directly or through better teacher design.  
4. Preserves teacher sovereignty over material decisions.  
5. Protects learner dignity (no labeling, no diagnosis, no ranking of children).  
6. Remains explainable where assistance influences outcomes.  
7. Is simple enough to use under classroom time pressure.  
8. Is accessible by default.  
9. Fails safely without destroying approved work.  
10. Can be defended under the Founding Charter and North Star ten years hence.

### 2.2 A feature is not “done” when it merely works
It is done when it is worthy: usable, ethical, maintainable, and educationally justified.

### 2.3 Negative definition
A capability that adds configuration without craft, automation without explanation, novelty without learning gain, or interface without a primary job **does not belong in PRIMA**—even if technically impressive.

# PART III — The Twelve Product Principles

Each principle is permanent. Each binds product, design, and engineering.

---

### Principle 1 — Pedagogy before technology
**Purpose.** Keep educational purpose in command.  
**Educational rationale.** Tools without pedagogy become noise.  
**Product implication.** Problem statements begin with teaching and learning, not with capabilities of systems.  
**Design implication.** Interface hierarchy foregrounds pedagogical decisions.  
**Engineering implication.** Architecture protects pedagogical invariants over convenient coupling.  
**Measure.** Feature brief cites educational problem and intended learning effect.

---

### Principle 2 — Teacher effort must decrease
**Purpose.** Honour scarce professional time.  
**Educational rationale.** Time recovered returns to learners.  
**Product implication.** Remove rewrite, search, and decision fatigue wherever Dual Value allows.  
**Design implication.** Defaults carry inclusive competence; advanced options stay progressive.  
**Engineering implication.** Performance and persistence protect continuity of work.  
**Measure.** Teachers complete the job with fewer steps or less cognitive burden than before.

---

### Principle 3 — Learning quality must increase
**Purpose.** Refuse empty efficiency.  
**Educational rationale.** Faster wrong teaching is still wrong.  
**Product implication.** Shortcuts that dilute objectives or validity require disclosure and teacher control.  
**Design implication.** Proof of preserved intention is visible at commitment moments.  
**Engineering implication.** Fidelity and validity checks are first-class outcomes, not afterthoughts.  
**Measure.** Commitment surfaces show objective fidelity or explicit uncertainty.

---

### Principle 4 — Complexity belongs behind the interface
**Purpose.** Protect teachers from instrumental complexity.  
**Educational rationale.** Cognitive load spent on tooling is stolen from pedagogy.  
**Product implication.** Orchestration, scoring, and branching stay invisible until needed.  
**Design implication.** One calm path; power through progressive disclosure.  
**Engineering implication.** Internal richness without external sprawl.  
**Measure.** New users identify the primary action within seconds on core screens.

---

### Principle 5 — Assistance explains before it insists
**Purpose.** Make counsel professional.  
**Educational rationale.** Unexplained influence cannot be owned.  
**Product implication.** Material recommendations carry what / why / benefit / trade-off.  
**Design implication.** Explanation is available at decision time, not buried.  
**Engineering implication.** Explainability artefacts travel with recommendations.  
**Measure.** Teachers can restate why a suggestion was made.

---

### Principle 6 — One action, one clear purpose
**Purpose.** Prevent fragmented attention.  
**Educational rationale.** Ambiguous screens produce hesitant craft.  
**Product implication.** Each screen and primary control has one job.  
**Design implication.** One dominant action; secondary actions visually quieter.  
**Engineering implication.** Flows avoid conflating unrelated state machines.  
**Measure.** Dual-value and single-job review pass before release.

---

### Principle 7 — Design for inclusion
**Purpose.** Make fair access ordinary.  
**Educational rationale.** Diversity of learning is strength.  
**Product implication.** Adapt resources; never label children; never diagnose.  
**Design implication.** Pathway language names supports, not deficits.  
**Engineering implication.** Inclusive workflows are core paths, not hidden modes.  
**Measure.** Inclusive adaptation is reachable without specialist configuration rituals.

---

### Principle 8 — Reduce cognitive load
**Purpose.** Keep minds free for teaching.  
**Educational rationale.** Overload degrades judgment.  
**Product implication.** Fewer simultaneous decisions; smarter defaults.  
**Design implication.** Short copy; clear hierarchy; restrained decoration.  
**Engineering implication.** Avoid interrupting teachers with nonessential alerts.  
**Measure.** Core tasks require fewer concurrent choices than prior baseline.

---

### Principle 9 — Consistency creates trust
**Purpose.** Make the studio learnable.  
**Educational rationale.** Predictability reduces anxiety and error.  
**Product implication.** Recurring patterns across modules.  
**Design implication.** Shared language, structure, and feedback behaviours.  
**Engineering implication.** Shared primitives preferred to one-off inventions.  
**Measure.** Cross-module pattern reuse documented; unjustified exceptions rejected.

---

### Principle 10 — Accessibility is a default
**Purpose.** Access is justice, not polish.  
**Educational rationale.** Teachers and learners vary in how they perceive and operate.  
**Product implication.** Critical paths are operable and intelligible from the start.  
**Design implication.** Focus, structure, contrast, and motion restraint are designed deliberately.  
**Engineering implication.** Access regressions block release.  
**Measure.** Critical paths pass accessibility gate criteria.

---

### Principle 11 — Progress over perfectionism theatre
**Purpose.** Ship worthy increments without false completeness.  
**Educational rationale.** Teachers need reliable help now, refined honestly over time.  
**Product implication.** Prefer a complete thin path that honours the Charter over a sprawling incomplete suite.  
**Design implication.** Honest “not yet” over deceptive fullness.  
**Engineering implication.** Vertical slices that are safe and teachable.  
**Measure.** Each release leaves a coherent teacher job completable end-to-end.

---

### Principle 12 — Every interaction must create value
**Purpose.** Eliminate empty motion.  
**Educational rationale.** Needless clicks tax vocation.  
**Product implication.** Each required step earns Dual Value or is removed.  
**Design implication.** Confirm only when risk warrants; never for ceremony.  
**Engineering implication.** No dead-end states; recovery is part of value.  
**Measure.** Feature review can justify every mandatory step; unjustified steps are cut.

# PART IV — The Teacher Experience Standard

Teachers using PRIMA should experience:

### 4.1 Confidence
They know what will happen next and why.  
**Product contribution:** Clear outcomes, honest states, reversible risk.

### 4.2 Clarity
They understand the job of each screen immediately.  
**Product contribution:** Single purpose, plain language, visible hierarchy.

### 4.3 Calm
The studio does not shout, crowd, or panic.  
**Product contribution:** Restraint, quiet waiting, non-punitive errors.

### 4.4 Control
They remain authors of pedagogical commitment.  
**Product contribution:** Accept / modify / reject; override; no forced automation.

### 4.5 Efficiency
Unnecessary labour falls away.  
**Product contribution:** Defaults, reuse, adaptation without rebuild.

### 4.6 Professional respect
Tone and structure honour vocation.  
**Product contribution:** Collegial copy; no shame scores; no deskilling rituals.

**Standard:** If a design produces confusion, coercion, or contempt—even subtly—it fails the Teacher Experience Standard.

---

# PART V — The Learner Impact Standard

Every feature must ultimately serve learners, usually **through** better teacher design.

### 5.1 Direct impact
Where PRIMA shapes materials learners meet: clearer access, dignified pathways, coherent tasks, fairer evidence of learning.

### 5.2 Indirect impact
Where PRIMA shapes teacher workflow: more time for feedback, better preparation, sustainable inclusive practice, reduced burnout that harms classrooms.

### 5.3 Non-impact that is unacceptable
Features that entertain builders, impress visitors, or automate appearance without improving access, understanding, fairness, or teacher craft **fail** this standard.

### 5.4 Dignity constraint
Learner benefit never justifies labeling, diagnosis, or ranking of children by the platform.

---

# PART VI — AI Product Philosophy

### 6.1 Assistance supports
Assisting intelligence proposes options and drafts that reduce rewrite burden.

### 6.2 Assistance never dominates
Interfaces are ateliers of decision, not theatres of automation.

### 6.3 Assistance remains transparent
Influence is visible; drafts are distinct from approved work.

### 6.4 Assistance explains
Material counsel includes rationale and trade-offs.

### 6.5 Assistance adapts resources, not children
Inclusive transformation targets materials and pathways.

### 6.6 Teacher remains responsible
Affirmation is human. Export and classroom commitment are human.

### 6.7 Silence is a product feature
When Dual Value is unclear, PRIMA does not invent urgency.

### 6.8 Refusal is healthy
Rejecting assistance never punishes the teacher.

# PART VII — Design Rules (mandatory)

1. **One primary objective per screen.**  
2. **One dominant action.**  
3. **Minimal cognitive load**—fewer simultaneous decisions.  
4. **Predictable navigation**—back means back; state remains coherent.  
5. **Visible feedback**—waiting, success, and failure are honest.  
6. **Accessible language**—plain, professional, non-clinical by default.  
7. **Forgiving interactions**—undo or recover where risk exists.  
8. **Progressive disclosure**—power behind calm defaults.  
9. **Dual value or redesign.**  
10. **No dark patterns**—no shame, trick urgency, or disguised commitment.  
11. **Consistent patterns**—new patterns require justification.  
12. **Empty, loading, and error states are designed**, not improvised.  
13. **Motion serves meaning**; never sole carrier of information.  
14. **Accessibility by default** on critical paths.  
15. **Explain material assistance at decision time.**

Violation of these rules returns the design to iteration.

# PART VIII — Feature Acceptance Criteria

Before approval, every feature must demonstrate:

| Criterion | Evidence of worthiness |
|-----------|------------------------|
| **Educational value** | Clear learning or teaching problem addressed |
| **Teacher value** | Effort ↓ or control ↑ without coercion |
| **Learner value** | Access, coherence, fairness, or depth ↑ (direct or via craft) |
| **Usability** | Primary job completable under time pressure |
| **Accessibility** | Critical path meets Accessibility Gate |
| **Maintainability** | Boundaries and clarity allow future stewardship |
| **Scalability** | Growth does not require abandoning Charter duties |
| **Explainability** | Material assistance is intelligible |
| **Trustworthiness** | Failures recoverable; approved work protected; privacy minimised |

Missing evidence blocks approval.

# PART IX — The Feature Review Framework

Every feature review must answer:

1. **What educational problem does it solve?**  
2. **Whose work becomes easier?**  
3. **How is learning improved?**  
4. **Can teachers understand it immediately?**  
5. **Can it be simplified further?**  
6. **Does assistance remain transparent and subordinate?**  
7. **Does it protect learner dignity?**  
8. **Does it align with the North Star and Charter?**  
9. **Would removing this feature improve the product?**

### Decision rule
- If (9) is **yes** → reject or redesign.  
- If any of (1)–(8) fails without remedy → return to design.  
- If Dual Value fails → return to design.

Reviews record answers. Rubber stamps are non-compliant.

# PART X — Anti-Patterns

PRIMA rejects:

1. **Feature bloat** — accumulation without Dual Value.  
2. **Technology without purpose** — capability looking for a problem.  
3. **Hidden automated decisions** — influence without disclosure.  
4. **Over-automation** — removing judgment teachers must keep.  
5. **Unnecessary configuration** — forms that feel like administration, not craft.  
6. **Interface clutter** — competing foci, badge noise, decorative distraction.  
7. **Dark patterns** — trick urgency, confirm-shaming, disguised commits.  
8. **Educational gimmicks** — novelty that does not improve learning.  
9. **Chat-shaped product capture** — replacing structured craft with aimless conversation as the primary path.  
10. **Content flooding** — volume without authorship or adaptivity.  
11. **Surveillance aesthetics** — dashboards that rank children or intimidate teachers.  
12. **Diagnostic drift** — clinical language or deficit labeling.  
13. **Metric theatre** — optimising vanity numbers against educational integrity.  
14. **Inconsistent micro-languages** — every module inventing its own grammar.  
15. **Unforgiving dead ends** — errors that strand work without recovery.  
16. **Accessibility as afterthought** — polish before access.  
17. **False completeness** — pretending unfinished paths are whole.  
18. **Punitive scoring of teachers** — treating professionals as defective outputs.

Encountering an anti-pattern is grounds for redesign, not for clever justification.

# PART XI — Definition of Product Excellence

Product excellence in PRIMA is **repeatable worthiness under professional constraint**.

### 11.1 Characteristics
- Teachers complete important jobs with calm confidence.  
- Learning opportunity widens without stigma.  
- Assistance is counsel, not command.  
- Complexity is borne by the system, not the teacher.  
- Failures are humane and recoverable.  
- Patterns are consistent enough to trust.  
- Access is default.  
- The feature still deserves existence years later.

### 11.2 Measurable signals (illustrative, not vanity)
- Time-to-complete core jobs ↓  
- Mandatory steps per job ↓ without learning quality ↓  
- Rate of understood recommendations ↑  
- Healthy modify/reject usage without coercion  
- Accessibility gate pass rate 100% on critical paths  
- Dual-value review pass before release  
- Incident rate of dignity/privacy violations → 0  

Numbers serve meaning. They never outrank the Charter.

# PART XII — The Product Promise

**To every teacher who enters PRIMA:**

We promise to treat your time as scarce and your judgment as sovereign.  
We promise instruments that make worthy learning design more possible, not more theatrical.  
We promise that assistance will explain itself, remain optional in spirit, and never seize your authorship.  
We promise to adapt resources for inclusion—and never to diminish learners with labels.  
We promise calm clarity over noise, access over ornament, and educational quality over haste.

If a feature cannot keep this promise, it does not belong in PRIMA.

This is our Product Promise.  
This is the foundation of all future product development.

---

# Closing

A feature belongs in PRIMA when it makes teaching more humanly possible and learning more justly reachable—under teacher control, with dignity intact.

Build only that.  
Refuse the rest.
