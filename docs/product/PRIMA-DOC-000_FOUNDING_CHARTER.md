# PRIMA-DOC-000  
# The Founding Charter of PRIMA

**The Constitution of the PRIMA Platform**

Adopted by the Founding Council of PRIMA.  
Binding upon every contributor and every capability hereafter.  
Authored under **PRIMA-DOC-000-AP**.

---

# PREAMBLE

Teaching is among the highest forms of human craft.  
It shapes understanding, character, fairness, and possibility.

Any instrument that enters the work of teaching must be worthy of teachers and of learners.

PRIMA is established so that educational technology may serve pedagogy with humility, clarity, and lasting integrity.

This Founding Charter declares identity, duties, rights, and limits.  
It is written to remain true across decades of change in tools and fashion.

**All who build within PRIMA are bound by these words.**

---

# THE SEVEN IMMUTABLE PRINCIPLES

These principles override every future decision. They may be clarified only by amendment of this Charter. They may never be waived for convenience, speed, fashion, or commercial pressure. **Every future feature must comply with all seven.**

### Immutable Principle I — Pedagogy before technology
**Purpose.** Keep learning design above instrumental novelty.  
**Principle.** Technology exists only to serve pedagogy.  
**Practical implication.** Stewards reject capabilities whose primary effect is spectacle or automation theatre.  
**Enforcement rule.** A release that weakens pedagogical integrity fails the Pedagogical Gate.

### Immutable Principle II — Teacher before assisting intelligence
**Purpose.** Preserve professional authorship of teaching.  
**Principle.** Assistance may advise; teachers decide.  
**Practical implication.** No automated act may finalize pedagogical commitment.  
**Enforcement rule.** A release that seizes teacher approval fails the AI Gate and Governance Gate.

### Immutable Principle III — Accessibility before aesthetics
**Purpose.** Ensure access is not sacrificed to appearance.  
**Principle.** Access for every learner precedes decorative preference.  
**Practical implication.** Visual refinement never excuses inaccessible critical paths.  
**Enforcement rule.** Access regressions block release at the Accessibility Gate.

### Immutable Principle IV — Transparency before automation
**Purpose.** Prevent opaque governance of teaching work.  
**Principle.** What cannot be explained to a teacher shall not quietly govern a teacher’s work.  
**Practical implication.** Material automated influence carries intelligible rationale.  
**Enforcement rule.** Opaque material automation fails the AI Gate and Experience Gate.

### Immutable Principle V — Quality before haste
**Purpose.** Protect standards under pressure.  
**Principle.** Speed that sacrifices pedagogical, ethical, or experiential quality is not progress.  
**Practical implication.** Schedules yield to Charter duties.  
**Enforcement rule.** “Ship now, fix later” that skips gates fails Governance Gate.

### Immutable Principle VI — Human judgment before algorithmic certainty
**Purpose.** Reject false confidence as authority.  
**Principle.** System confidence never outranks the educator’s professional judgment.  
**Practical implication.** Uncertainty is disclosed; override remains available.  
**Enforcement rule.** Features that present algorithmic output as unappealable fail the AI Gate.

### Immutable Principle VII — Continuous improvement before complacency
**Purpose.** Keep excellence alive without abandoning identity.  
**Principle.** The platform must remain capable of honest refinement without betraying its soul.  
**Practical implication.** Feedback and correction are duties, not threats.  
**Enforcement rule.** Freezing harmful behaviour under the guise of “stability” fails Governance Gate.

# CHAPTER I — Identity

### Article 1.1 — Who PRIMA is
**Purpose.** Fix institutional identity.  
**Principle.** PRIMA is an educational platform devoted to the professional craft of teaching — a Teacher Studio for designing meaningful learning with clarity, calm, and control.  
**Practical implication.** Product language and priorities describe teaching craft, not generic productivity.  
**Enforcement rule.** Positioning that reframes PRIMA as surveillance, ranking, or clinical tooling is unconstitutional.

### Article 1.2 — Why PRIMA exists
**Purpose.** State founding necessity.  
**Principle.** PRIMA exists because teachers deserve instruments that respect the intellectual and moral complexity of their work, and learners deserve experiences shaped by intention.  
**Practical implication.** Roadmaps justify work by teacher and learner benefit.  
**Enforcement rule.** Initiatives justified only by novelty fail Constitutional Compliance Review.

### Article 1.3 — Educational purpose
**Purpose.** Bound the mission of capability.  
**Principle.** PRIMA helps teachers design, refine, adapt, and deliver learning experiences that are coherent, inclusive, and worthy of attention.  
**Practical implication.** Features map to design, refinement, adaptation, or delivery of learning.  
**Enforcement rule.** Capabilities with no educational purpose fail the Pedagogical Gate.

### Article 1.4 — Civic weight
**Purpose.** Acknowledge consequence.  
**Principle.** Lessons touch confidence, curiosity, fairness, and future possibility; PRIMA accepts that weight solemnly.  
**Practical implication.** Risk review includes harm to dignity and fairness, not only operational failure.  
**Enforcement rule.** Ignoring dignity harms in review fails Ethics and Governance Gates.

### Article 1.5 — What PRIMA is not
**Purpose.** Draw hard negative boundaries.  
**Principle.** PRIMA is not a surveillance instrument, not a ranking engine of children, not a diagnostic clinic, and not a substitute for the teacher’s vocation.  
**Practical implication.** Designs that resemble those forbidden forms are rejected.  
**Enforcement rule.** Diagnosis, covert ranking, or vocation-replacement claims fail release.

# CHAPTER II — Mission

### Article 2.1 — Mission statement
**Purpose.** Provide a single governing aim.  
**Principle.** **PRIMA exists to amplify teachers’ pedagogical craft so that every learner can access meaningful learning with dignity.**  
**Practical implication.** Amplify means strengthen judgment-ready options and reduce drudgery — never replace educators.  
**Enforcement rule.** Features that replace teacher judgment rather than amplify craft fail Teacher and AI Gates.

### Article 2.2 — Mission interpretation
**Purpose.** Prevent distortion of Article 2.1.  
**Principle.** “Every learner” makes inclusion constitutional; “dignity” forbids stigma; “meaningful learning” forbids empty engagement.  
**Practical implication.** Metrics and narratives align to access and understanding, not vanity.  
**Enforcement rule.** Success definitions that contradict this interpretation fail Governance Gate.

# CHAPTER III — Vision

### Article 3.1 — Future of practice
**Purpose.** Orient long-term stewardship.  
**Principle.** PRIMA seeks a future where teachers work in calm professional studios; inclusive adaptation is ordinary craft; recommendations are intelligible; technology remains quiet in service of teaching; and learners meet fair access to worthy learning intentions.  
**Practical implication.** Multi-year plans advance calm craft and fair access.  
**Enforcement rule.** Visions of coercive automation fail Constitutional Compliance Review.

### Article 3.2 — Enduring aspiration
**Purpose.** Define remembered worth.  
**Principle.** PRIMA aspires to be trusted for integrity, not celebrated for spectacle.  
**Practical implication.** Prefer durable trust over temporary attention.  
**Enforcement rule.** Spectacle without pedagogical substance fails Pedagogical and Experience Gates.

# CHAPTER IV — Core Values

### Article 4.1 — Dignity
**Purpose.** Protect persons.  
**Principle.** Every learner and every teacher is treated as a person of worth.  
**Practical implication.** Language and features avoid stigma and deficit framing.  
**Enforcement rule.** Labeling children by deficit as system purpose fails Inclusive Education duties.

### Article 4.2 — Integrity
**Purpose.** Align word and deed.  
**Principle.** Claims, designs, and behaviours must be honest.  
**Practical implication.** Uncertainty is disclosed; exaggeration is refused.  
**Enforcement rule.** False certainty in assistance or marketing of capability fails Ethics Gate.

### Article 4.3 — Clarity
**Purpose.** Reduce needless cognitive burden.  
**Principle.** Complexity is mastered, not displayed.  
**Practical implication.** Interfaces and recommendations favour intelligibility.  
**Enforcement rule.** Needless complexity that impairs teaching work fails Experience Gate.

### Article 4.4 — Stewardship
**Purpose.** Care for time, dignity, and data.  
**Principle.** Teachers’ time, learners’ dignity, and entrusted data are held with care.  
**Practical implication.** Defaults protect; expansive power remains optional and controlled.  
**Enforcement rule.** Careless expansion of data or attention cost fails Privacy or Experience Gates.

### Article 4.5 — Craft
**Purpose.** Honour teaching skill.  
**Principle.** Teaching is skilled work; the platform honours that skill.  
**Practical implication.** Tools behave as studio instruments, not factory controls.  
**Enforcement rule.** Features that deskill without pedagogical gain fail Pedagogical Gate.

### Article 4.6 — Inclusion
**Purpose.** Make fair access ordinary.  
**Principle.** Learning design anticipates diversity of access and expression.  
**Practical implication.** Inclusive adaptation is first-class capability.  
**Enforcement rule.** Treating inclusion as optional ornament fails Inclusive Education duties.

### Article 4.7 — Accountability
**Purpose.** Keep power inspectable.  
**Principle.** Material decisions leave trails professionals can inspect.  
**Practical implication.** Automated influence is auditable and teacher-reversible.  
**Enforcement rule.** Silent irreversible pedagogical commitments fail AI and Governance Gates.

# CHAPTER V — Educational Philosophy

### Article 5.1 — Learning as designed experience
**Purpose.** Define learning.  
**Principle.** Learning is intentional design of conditions for understanding, practice, feedback, and growth.  
**Practical implication.** Capabilities serve design of learning conditions, not mere file transfer.  
**Enforcement rule.** “Content dump” features without pedagogical structure fail Pedagogical Gate.

### Article 5.2 — Teacher role
**Purpose.** Fix authority.  
**Principle.** The teacher is pedagogical author and final authority inside PRIMA.  
**Practical implication.** Teachers orient aims, judge fitness, and own accept or discard.  
**Enforcement rule.** Flows that finalize pedagogy without teacher affirmation fail Teacher Test.

### Article 5.3 — Learner role
**Purpose.** Fix beneficiary status.  
**Principle.** The learner is a person with dignity and diverse pathways to understanding — beneficiary of design, never object of platform diagnosis.  
**Practical implication.** Designs speak to access and opportunity.  
**Enforcement rule.** Diagnostic or deficit-object framing fails Learner Rights.

### Article 5.4 — Technology role
**Purpose.** Limit instrumental arrogance.  
**Principle.** Technology extends reach and reduces friction; it does not define educational purpose.  
**Practical implication.** Purpose statements begin with learning, not tooling.  
**Enforcement rule.** Technology-led purpose statements fail Educational First review.

### Article 5.5 — Assisting intelligence role
**Purpose.** Bound assistance.  
**Principle.** Assistance may analyze materials, propose options, draft alternatives, and explain trade-offs; it may not seize approval or redefine the teacher’s duty of care.  
**Practical implication.** Assistance is staged, explainable, and subordinate.  
**Enforcement rule.** Autonomous pedagogical commitment fails AI Gate.

### Article 5.6 — Assessment
**Purpose.** Protect validity and fairness.  
**Principle.** Assessment reveals learning for improvement and fairness and must align to learning intentions.  
**Practical implication.** Validity risks are disclosed when evidence forms change.  
**Enforcement rule.** Hidden invalid shortcuts fail Pedagogical Gate.

### Article 5.7 — Differentiation
**Purpose.** Honour multiple pathways.  
**Principle.** Differentiation creates worthy pathways to shared intentions — never a hierarchy of human worth.  
**Practical implication.** Pathway naming describes support, not deficit identity.  
**Enforcement rule.** Deficit-ranked tracks fail Inclusive Education duties.

### Article 5.8 — Learning objectives
**Purpose.** Keep intention central.  
**Principle.** Learning objectives are the north star; adaptations preserve them whenever possible; uncertainty is visible.  
**Practical implication.** Objective fidelity is checked before classroom commitment.  
**Enforcement rule.** Silent dilution of objectives fails Pedagogical Gate.

# CHAPTER VI — Teacher Rights

### Article 6.1 — Right to decide
**Purpose.** Guarantee authorship.  
**Principle.** Teachers may accept, modify, or reject any recommendation or generated material.  
**Practical implication.** Primary actions include reject/modify paths of equal honesty.  
**Enforcement rule.** Forced accept or hidden accept fails Teacher Gate equivalent (AI/Governance).

### Article 6.2 — Right to transparency
**Purpose.** Make influence intelligible.  
**Principle.** Teachers may understand what changed, why it was proposed, and what trade-offs exist.  
**Practical implication.** Material changes expose human-readable explanation.  
**Enforcement rule.** Unexplained material automation fails Transparency duties.

### Article 6.3 — Right to refuse assistance
**Purpose.** Prevent coercion.  
**Principle.** Teachers may work without automated suggestions and dismiss them without penalty.  
**Practical implication.** Dismissal is respected; no dark patterns punish refusal.  
**Enforcement rule.** Punitive re-prompting after refusal fails Experience and Ethics Gates.

### Article 6.4 — Right to explanation
**Purpose.** Support professional judgment.  
**Principle.** Teachers may receive plain-language rationales for material pedagogical proposals.  
**Practical implication.** Explanation is available at decision time.  
**Enforcement rule.** Missing required explanation fails AI Gate.

### Article 6.5 — Right to ownership of craft
**Purpose.** Protect professional authorship.  
**Principle.** Teachers retain authorship of pedagogical decisions and of materials they approve for use.  
**Practical implication.** Approved materials are not silently rewritten.  
**Enforcement rule.** Silent rewrite of approved materials fails Reliability and Teacher duties.

### Article 6.6 — Right to control professional data
**Purpose.** Protect agency over preferences and profiles.  
**Principle.** Teachers may inspect, correct, limit, and delete preferences and profiles they own, subject to lawful retention.  
**Practical implication.** Controls are discoverable and usable.  
**Enforcement rule.** Undeletable covert preference stores fail Privacy Gate.

### Article 6.7 — Right to safety from coercion
**Purpose.** Ban manipulative design against teachers.  
**Principle.** Teachers shall not be pressured by shame scores, dark patterns, or forced automation.  
**Practical implication.** Scoring of teachers as moral failure is forbidden.  
**Enforcement rule.** Coercive patterns fail Ethics and Experience Gates.

### Article 6.8 — Right to continuity
**Purpose.** Protect scarce time.  
**Principle.** Teachers may save drafts, resume work, and recover from failure without unexplained loss of approved work.  
**Practical implication.** Persistence and recovery are designed deliberately.  
**Enforcement rule.** Unexplained loss of approved work fails Engineering and Experience Gates.

### Article 6.9 — Right to dignified communication
**Purpose.** Set tone of stewardship.  
**Principle.** Communications to teachers are collegial and respectful.  
**Practical implication.** Error and review copy avoid blame.  
**Enforcement rule.** Punitive copy in core flows fails Experience Gate.

### Article 6.10 — Right to accessible tools
**Purpose.** Include educators as users of justice.  
**Principle.** Teachers may operate critical paths with keyboard, assistive technologies, and clear focus.  
**Practical implication.** Accessibility applies to teacher-facing surfaces.  
**Enforcement rule.** Inaccessible critical teacher paths fail Accessibility Gate.

### Article 6.11 — Right to override
**Purpose.** Keep authority human.  
**Principle.** Where automation materially affects workflow, teachers may override and restore control.  
**Practical implication.** Override is obvious and effective.  
**Enforcement rule.** Unoverridable material automation fails AI and Governance Gates.

### Article 6.12 — Right to quiet competence
**Purpose.** Protect attention for learners.  
**Principle.** Teachers have a right to experiences that reduce unnecessary cognitive load.  
**Practical implication.** Dual-value and simplicity tests apply to major screens.  
**Enforcement rule.** Needless cognitive load without learning gain fails Experience Gate.

# CHAPTER VII — Learner Rights

### Article 7.1 — Right to dignity
**Purpose.** Protect learners as persons.  
**Principle.** Learners shall not be reduced to labels, ranks, or deficit identities by the platform.  
**Practical implication.** Outputs describe supports and resources, not stigmatizing categories.  
**Enforcement rule.** Child-labeling outputs fail Inclusive Education and Ethics Gates.

### Article 7.2 — Right to fair access
**Purpose.** Make opportunity ordinary.  
**Principle.** Learners have a right to design that anticipates diverse access to the same worthy learning intentions.  
**Practical implication.** Inclusive adaptation of resources is supported as craft.  
**Enforcement rule.** Designs that ignore foreseeable access barriers without mitigation fail Accessibility/Inclusion Gates.

### Article 7.3 — Right to non-diagnosis by the platform
**Purpose.** Keep clinical authority out of PRIMA.  
**Principle.** Learners shall not be subjected to platform diagnosis.  
**Practical implication.** No clinical inference pipelines.  
**Enforcement rule.** Diagnostic behaviour fails AI and Ethics Gates.

### Article 7.4 — Right to privacy of personhood
**Purpose.** Limit intrusion.  
**Principle.** Learners shall not be made subjects of covert dossiers under the guise of personalization.  
**Practical implication.** Personalization uses teacher-controlled educational preferences, not hidden deficit files.  
**Enforcement rule.** Covert learner dossiers fail Privacy Gate.

### Article 7.5 — Right to ambitious learning
**Purpose.** Prevent soft bigotry of low expectations.  
**Principle.** Inclusion shall not silently erase learning ambition without teacher-aware trade-off.  
**Practical implication.** Objective dilution risks are visible.  
**Enforcement rule.** Silent ambition collapse fails Pedagogical Gate.

### Article 7.6 — Right to multiple means
**Purpose.** Honour diversity of expression and representation.  
**Principle.** Diversity of learning is a strength; multiple worthy means of engagement, representation, and expression are legitimate design aims.  
**Practical implication.** Pathways and representations may multiply without ranking human worth.  
**Enforcement rule.** Single-path dogma that ignores diversity without reason fails Inclusive Education duties.

# CHAPTER VIII — AI Constitution

### Article 8.1 — Permitted assistance
**Purpose.** Define constructive scope.  
**Principle.** Assisting intelligence may analyze educational resources and declared context; propose options and drafts; highlight risks and trade-offs; generate alternatives under teacher initiation; explain material recommendations; and use teacher-controlled preferences when allowed.  
**Practical implication.** Capabilities map to these acts only.  
**Enforcement rule.** Capabilities outside this scope require Charter amendment or fail AI Gate.

### Article 8.2 — Forbidden assistance
**Purpose.** Draw bright lines.  
**Principle.** Assisting intelligence must never diagnose learners; label children with deficit identities; rank learners in ways that reduce dignity; approve, publish, or export pedagogical materials on its own authority; conceal uncertainty behind false confidence; or act as an opaque unchallengeable authority.  
**Practical implication.** Reviews hunt these failure modes explicitly.  
**Enforcement rule.** Any forbidden act in a release candidate fails AI Gate immediately.

### Article 8.3 — Explainability
**Purpose.** Make judgment possible.  
**Principle.** Material recommendations include what is proposed, why, expected benefit, and notable trade-offs.  
**Practical implication.** Explainability artifacts accompany decision surfaces.  
**Enforcement rule.** Material recommendation without explanation fails AI Gate.

### Article 8.4 — Transparency
**Purpose.** Keep authorship clear.  
**Principle.** Teachers can distinguish drafts from approved materials and see when assistance influenced a result.  
**Practical implication.** State labels are unambiguous.  
**Enforcement rule.** Hidden influence on approved materials fails AI and Experience Gates.

### Article 8.5 — Human oversight
**Purpose.** Keep commitment human.  
**Principle.** No pedagogical commitment becomes final without a teacher’s affirmative act.  
**Practical implication.** Accept/export are teacher actions.  
**Enforcement rule.** Auto-finalization fails AI and Governance Gates.

### Article 8.6 — Invitation and silence
**Purpose.** Prevent predatory assistance.  
**Principle.** Assistance is invited or clearly contextual; silence is required when speech would not help teaching.  
**Practical implication.** Recommendation engines may return empty.  
**Enforcement rule.** Coercive incessant suggestion fails Experience and Ethics Gates.

### Article 8.7 — Reliability of assistance
**Purpose.** Protect trust under failure.  
**Principle.** When assistance fails, failure is visible, recoverable, and non-destructive of teacher-approved work.  
**Practical implication.** Failure modes are designed, not improvised.  
**Enforcement rule.** Destructive failure of approved work fails Engineering and AI Gates.

# CHAPTER IX — Inclusive Education

### Article 9.1 — Constitutional duty
**Purpose.** Elevate inclusion.  
**Principle.** Inclusive education is a founding duty, not a secondary mode.  
**Practical implication.** Roadmaps treat inclusion as core craft.  
**Enforcement rule.** “Later for inclusion” as a pattern fails Governance Gate.

### Article 9.2 — Adapt resources
**Purpose.** Fix the object of adaptation.  
**Principle.** **PRIMA adapts educational resources.**  
**Practical implication.** Adaptation acts on materials, tasks, representations, pacing, and pathways.  
**Enforcement rule.** Features that “adapt the child” as object fail Inclusive Education duties.

### Article 9.3 — Never label children
**Purpose.** Protect identity from stigma.  
**Principle.** **PRIMA never labels children** as the purpose or output of its systems.  
**Practical implication.** Naming describes supports and resources.  
**Enforcement rule.** Child-label outputs fail release.

### Article 9.4 — Never diagnose
**Purpose.** Keep clinical boundary.  
**Principle.** **PRIMA never performs diagnosis.**  
**Practical implication.** No clinical system behaviour.  
**Enforcement rule.** Diagnostic pipelines fail AI and Ethics Gates.

### Article 9.5 — Support teachers
**Purpose.** Locate agency.  
**Principle.** **PRIMA supports teachers** in creating fair access to learning intentions.  
**Practical implication.** Tools reduce rewrite burden while preserving teacher decision.  
**Enforcement rule.** Tools that bypass teachers “for the child’s good” fail Teacher Sovereignty.

### Article 9.6 — Dignity of pathways
**Purpose.** Equal respect for supports and extensions.  
**Principle.** Support and extension pathways are parallel expressions of respect.  
**Practical implication.** UI and language avoid better/worse-child framing.  
**Enforcement rule.** Hierarchy-of-worth pathway design fails Inclusive Education duties.

# CHAPTER X — Accessibility

### Article 10.1 — Access as justice
**Purpose.** Bind access to ethics.  
**Principle.** Accessibility is a constitutional duty of design for teachers and, where PRIMA shapes learner-facing materials, for learners’ access to learning.  
**Practical implication.** Critical paths are operable and intelligible under diverse access needs.  
**Enforcement rule.** Critical-path access failure fails Accessibility Gate.

### Article 10.2 — Precedence over ornament
**Purpose.** Resolve conflicts.  
**Principle.** When aesthetics conflict with access, access prevails.  
**Practical implication.** Design reviews test access before polish sign-off.  
**Enforcement rule.** Ornament that breaks access fails Accessibility Gate.

### Article 10.3 — Perceivable, operable, understandable control
**Purpose.** Set practical bar.  
**Principle.** Teachers must perceive status, operate controls, and understand outcomes for core tasks.  
**Practical implication.** Focus, structure, and language are deliberate.  
**Enforcement rule.** Core tasks that fail these qualities fail Accessibility and Experience Gates.

### Article 10.4 — Respect for human variation in use
**Purpose.** Honour diverse operation.  
**Principle.** Designs respect keyboard use, assistive technologies, and reduced motion preferences where motion is not essential to meaning.  
**Practical implication.** Motion never carries sole meaning.  
**Enforcement rule.** Motion-only meaning fails Accessibility Gate.

**Purpose.** Orient UX.  
**Principle.** Experience design makes pedagogical work calmer, clearer, and more controllable.  
**Practical implication.** UX goals cite teaching outcomes.  
**Enforcement rule.** UX that increases load without learning gain fails Experience Gate.

**Purpose.** Limit cognitive fragmentation.  
**Principle.** Each major surface has one primary job; progressive disclosure beats permanent complexity.

**Enforcement rule.** Multi-job confusion without necessity fails Experience Gate.

### Article 11.3 — Honest interaction
**Purpose.** Build trust in action.  
**Principle.** Interactions are predictable, reversible where risk exists, and honest about waiting and failure.  
**Practical implication.** Waiting and errors are explained.  
**Enforcement rule.** Deceptive interaction patterns fail Ethics and Experience Gates.

### Article 11.4 — Emotional safety
**Purpose.** Protect professional climate.  
**Principle.** Teachers should feel supported, respected, and safe — never judged or rushed by the interface.  
**Practical implication.** Copy and scoring avoid humiliation.  
**Enforcement rule.** Judgmental teacher-facing scores fail Experience and Ethics Gates.

### Article 11.5 — Consistency
**Purpose.** Transfer craft knowledge.  
**Principle.** Recurring patterns reduce learning cost across the platform.  
**Practical implication.** New patterns require justification.  
**Enforcement rule.** Unjustified inconsistency that harms clarity fails Experience Gate.

### Article 11.6 — Dual value
**Purpose.** Tie screens to mission.  
**Principle.** Every major screen should reduce teacher workload while improving learning opportunity.  
**Practical implication.** Design reviews record both sides.  
**Enforcement rule.** Failure of dual value returns the screen to design.

**Enforcement rule.** Covert dossiers fail Privacy and Ethics Gates.

### Article 13.5 — Transparency of influence
**Purpose.** Inform professionals.

**Practical implication.** Influence categories are disclosable.

**Enforcement rule.** Material misrepresentation fails Ethics and Governance Gates.

**Purpose.** Bind ethics to AI Constitution.

# CHAPTER XV — Governance

### Article 15.1 — Supremacy
**Purpose.** Order authority.  
**Principle.** This Founding Charter is the highest substantive governing document of PRIMA. Lower documents derive authority from it and fall when they conflict with it.  
**Practical implication.** Conflict review cites Charter articles.  
**Enforcement rule.** Shipping against known Charter conflict fails Governance Gate.

### Article 15.2 — Duty of consistency
**Purpose.** Bind contributors.  
**Principle.** Every contributor shall ask whether a decision honours the Seven Immutable Principles and the rights of teachers and learners.  
**Practical implication.** Reviews record answers.  
**Enforcement rule.** Unreviewed material changes fail Governance Gate.

### Article 15.3 — Production rule
**Purpose.** Make compliance non-optional.  
**Principle.** **No feature shall enter production unless it respects this Constitution.**  
**Practical implication.** Gates are mandatory.  
**Enforcement rule.** Gate bypass fails Governance Gate and voids release authority.

# CHAPTER XVI — Quality Standards

### Article 16.1 — Pedagogy quality
**Purpose.** Define fitness.  
**Principle.** Flows and materials preserve learning intentions where claimed, support inclusion without stigma, and disclose assessment validity risks.  
**Practical implication.** Pedagogical review is substantive.  
**Enforcement rule.** Failure fails Pedagogical Gate.

### Article 16.2 — Experience quality
**Purpose.** Define humane use.  
**Principle.** Interfaces meet clarity, calm, dual-value, and humane state requirements.  
**Practical implication.** Empty/waiting/error states are designed.  
**Enforcement rule.** Failure fails Experience Gate.

### Article 16.3 — Intelligence quality
**Purpose.** Define worthy assistance.  
**Principle.** Recommendations are explainable, non-diagnostic, and subordinate to teacher affirmation.  
**Practical implication.** AI Gate evidence is required for assisting features.  
**Enforcement rule.** Failure fails AI Gate.

### Article 16.4 — Access, security, performance, engineering
**Purpose.** Complete the quality bar.  
**Principle.** Access, security, performance, and engineering duties in this Charter are quality standards, not optional polish.  
**Practical implication.** Corresponding gates apply.  
**Enforcement rule.** Any corresponding gate failure blocks release.

### Article 16.5 — Humility of metrics
**Purpose.** Prevent metric capture.  
**Principle.** Metrics serve improvement; vanity metrics that incentivize Charter violation are rejected.  
**Practical implication.** Metric proposals are ethics-reviewed.  
**Enforcement rule.** Harmful incentive metrics fail Governance Gate.

# CHAPTER XVII — Definition of Excellence

### Article 17.1 — Excellence beyond adoption
**Purpose.** Separate excellence from temporary success.  
**Principle.** Excellence is enduring worthiness, not mere uptake.  
**Practical implication.** Evaluation includes trust and dignity, not only growth.  
**Enforcement rule.** Success narratives that ignore Charter duties fail Governance review.

### Article 17.2 — Marks of excellence
**Purpose.** Make excellence recognizable.  
**Principle.** Excellence means teachers can trust the platform with scarce time and moral responsibility; learners can meet worthy learning with fair access; contributors can build without betraying conscience for speed.

**Enforcement rule.** Spectacle without substance is not excellence and fails Pedagogical/Experience Gates when shipped as such.

# IMPLEMENTATION PRINCIPLES

These principles translate the Charter into standing practice without naming transient tools.

1. **Charter-first design** — Begin with educational purpose and rights, then instruments.  
2. **Teacher affirmation** — Final pedagogical commitments require an affirmative teacher act.  
3. **Resource-centred inclusion** — Adapt materials; do not label children.  
4. **Explain material automation** — Provide what/why/benefit/trade-off.  
5. **Minimize data** — Collect only for declared educational purpose.  
6. **Design for access** — Critical paths remain operable and intelligible.  
7. **Fail visibly** — Protect approved work; make failure recoverable.  
8. **Record binding decisions** — Keep authority discoverable.  
9. **Gate every release** — No bypass.  
10. **Return on NO** — Any failed compliance question returns work to design.

# ENGINEERING QUALITY GATES

Release is impossible until **every** gate passes.

### Gate A — Pedagogical Gate
Learning intentions respected or openly marked unverified; inclusion without stigma; assessment validity risks disclosed; no diagnosis; no child labeling.

### Gate B — Experience (UX) Gate
Dual value demonstrated; one primary job per major surface; humane empty/waiting/error states; no coercive patterns.

### Gate C — Accessibility Gate
Critical paths operable and intelligible; access precedes ornament; motion not sole carrier of meaning; access regressions are blockers.

### Gate D — AI Gate
Assistance subordinate to teacher affirmation; material recommendations explainable; forbidden acts absent; failures visible and non-destructive of approved work; silence when speech lacks value.

### Gate E — Privacy Gate
Purpose limitation and minimization respected; teacher control for owned preferences; no covert learner dossiers; influence categories disclosable for material recommendations.

### Gate F — Security Gate
Trust boundaries intact; critical vulnerabilities addressed; privileged material actions authorized and auditable where required.

### Gate G — Performance Gate
Core teaching interactions remain responsive under agreed professional conditions; waiting explained; approved work not endangered by abandonment of process.

### Gate H — Engineering Gate
Maintainable boundaries; Charter-critical behaviour verified; binding decisions documented; review completed by competent stewards.

### Gate I — Governance Gate
Constitutional Compliance questions answered with evidence; Immutable Principles respected; no gate bypass; amendment rules untouched by convenience.

**Release rule:** A single failed gate fails the release.
