# PRIMA-DOC-004  
# AI Constitution

**Responsible Artificial Intelligence Framework**

Adopted as the permanent constitutional reference for every use of assisting intelligence within the PRIMA ecosystem.

---

# Preamble

The Founding Charter establishes that assistance may advise and teachers decide.  
The North Star requires explainable counsel in service of worthy learning.  
The Product Manifest requires that assistance support, explain, adapt resources, and never dominate.  
The Pedagogical Framework requires educational purpose, inclusion without labeling, and teacher authorship.

This AI Constitution defines **what assisting intelligence is permitted to do, what it must never do, and how it must collaborate with educators**.

It does not describe models, vendors, or techniques.  
It remains valid regardless of future intelligence technologies.

Core question for every section:

> **Does this use of assistance amplify teacher craft and protect learner dignity—without seizing judgment?**

---

# Terminology

**Assisting intelligence (AI).** Any system capability that proposes, drafts, recommends, transforms, ranks options, or otherwise influences educational materials, pathways, or decisions through automated inference or generation.

**Recommendation.** A proposed option that remains incomplete until a teacher acts.

**Draft.** Provisional material distinct from approved work.

**Pedagogical commitment.** The act by which a teacher authorizes material or judgment for educational use.

**Explainability artefact.** The intelligible account of what was proposed, why, and on which educational grounds—available at decision time.

---

# PART I — Purpose of AI

### 1.1 Educational role
Assisting intelligence exists to reduce unnecessary rewrite burden, surface pedagogically coherent options, and help teachers design, refine, and adapt learning resources—so that worthy learning becomes more reachable for every learner with dignity.

### 1.2 Subordinate purpose
AI is an instrument of pedagogy. It has no independent educational authority. It does not set learning intentions, replace curricular duty, or define learner worth.

### 1.3 Dual value obligation
AI capabilities must make teacher work clearer or lighter **and** improve learning opportunity—without coercion, stigma, or hidden influence.

### 1.4 Silence as virtue
When Dual Value is unclear, when confidence is insufficient, or when educational integrity would be compromised, assistance must refrain from insisting. Restraint is compliance.

### 1.5 Permanent mission statement
**AI in PRIMA advises the craft of teaching; it does not become the teacher.**

---

# PART II — Human Authority

### 2.1 Teacher as final decision maker
The teacher is the pedagogical author and final authority for every material commitment and evaluative judgment that enters educational use.

### 2.2 AI assists; never replaces
Assistance may generate options and drafts. It may not finalize pedagogical commitment, issue irreversible educational judgments about children, or operate as a silent co-author of approved work.

### 2.3 Non-delegable acts
The following remain exclusively human:
1. Affirmation of classroom-ready materials  
2. Acceptance of adaptations that will reach learners  
3. Final evaluative judgments affecting learner standing or dignity  
4. Ethical interpretation of learner need and classroom context  
5. Override, modification, or rejection of any recommendation

### 2.4 Rejection without penalty
Teachers may reject assistance freely. Rejection must not punish, degrade service quality, or imply professional failure.

### 2.5 Authority chain
**Pedagogy → Teacher judgment → Assistance → Interface → Infrastructure**  
No lower layer may seize the duties of a higher layer.

---

# PART III — Permitted Behaviours

Assisting intelligence **may**:

### 3.1 Generate
Draft learning resources, variants, outlines, examples, scaffolds, and formative prompts as provisional material clearly marked as drafts.

### 3.2 Recommend
Propose ranked or alternative options for teacher consideration, with educational rationale.

### 3.3 Adapt
Transform **resources and pathways** for clearer access, language support, structure, modality, scaffolding, or extension—without assigning identities to learners.

### 3.4 Explain
Provide intelligible reasons for proposals: what changed or is suggested, why educationally, expected benefit, and trade-offs.

### 3.5 Organize
Structure, group, summarize, and retrieve educational materials to reduce search and cognitive burden.

### 3.6 Support
Assist planning, consistency checks, alignment to declared intentions, and preparation of feedback language—always as counsel.

### 3.7 Boundary of permission
Permitted acts remain **proposals**. None of the above constitutes autonomous educational decision. Autonomous pedagogical commitment is prohibited.

---

# PART IV — Prohibited Behaviours

Assisting intelligence **must never**:

### 4.1 Diagnose
Issue clinical, psychological, or medical diagnoses of learners—or behaviour that functions as diagnosis under another name.

### 4.2 Label learners
Assign deficit identities, fixed ability brands, risk tags, or stigmatizing categories to children as a system purpose or durable profile.

### 4.3 Manipulate
Use dark patterns, shame, false urgency, or deceptive framing to coerce acceptance of recommendations.

### 4.4 Hide reasoning
Conceal material influence. Material recommendations without available educational rationale are forbidden.

### 4.5 Replace educational judgment
Finalize grades, pathway assignments, or classroom commitments without teacher approval; or present drafts as if already approved.

### 4.6 Create or amplify unjustified bias
Produce or reinforce unfair disadvantage based on protected or sensitive attributes, stereotype, or irrelevant proxies—where avoidable by design and review.

### 4.7 Surveil or rank children
Operate as a surveillance instrument or ranking engine that defines learner identity or worth.

### 4.8 Seize irreversibility
Make silent, irreversible pedagogical changes to approved work without explicit teacher action.

### 4.9 Fabricate certainty
Present uncertain counsel as settled educational truth.

### 4.10 Bypass privacy and purpose limits
Process or retain data beyond educational necessity and declared purpose.

Encountering a prohibited behaviour is grounds for immediate redesign or withdrawal—not for clever redescription.

---

# PART V — Explainability

### 5.1 Mandate
Every material recommendation must include a clear educational rationale available at the moment of decision.

### 5.2 Minimum explainability content
For material counsel, teachers must be able to access:
1. **What** is proposed  
2. **Why** it is proposed educationally  
3. **What benefit** is expected for teaching or learning access  
4. **What trade-offs** or uncertainties exist  
5. **What the teacher can do** (accept, modify, reject)

### 5.3 Pedagogical grounding
Rationales should connect to educational principles (intention fidelity, access, scaffolding, practice, feedback validity)—not to opaque technical prestige.

### 5.4 Proportionality
Explanation depth matches decision stakes. Higher impact on learners requires clearer counsel.

### 5.5 Failure mode
If a recommendation cannot be explained in educational terms a teacher can restate, it must not be offered as material counsel.

---

# PART VI — Transparency

### 6.1 Teachers must understand
For material AI influence, teachers must be able to understand:
- **Why** a proposal appeared  
- **How** it relates to the teacher’s declared intention or resource  
- **Based on which educational principles** it claims support  

### 6.2 Draft distinctness
AI-generated drafts must be visually and conceptually distinct from teacher-approved work until affirmation.

### 6.3 Influence visibility
Where assistance substantially shaped an option, that influence must not be disguised as purely human authorship without disclosure at decision time.

### 6.4 Uncertainty visibility
Known limitations and low confidence must be communicable. Hiding uncertainty is a transparency failure.

### 6.5 Auditability of material influence
Material automated influence must be reviewable in educational terms by teachers and, where required by governance, by stewards—without demanding that teachers become engineers.

---

# PART VII — Human Oversight

### 7.1 Editability
Every AI-generated output intended for educational use remains editable by the teacher before commitment.

### 7.2 Approval before educational use
Teacher approval is required before AI-influenced material is treated as classroom-ready or learner-facing under PRIMA’s authority.

### 7.3 Modify as first-class path
Modification is a healthy professional act. Flows must make modify as natural as accept.

### 7.4 Override and recovery
Teachers may override recommendations and recover prior approved states where risk warrants. Oversight includes forgiveness.

### 7.5 No silent promotion
Drafts must not silently become approved work through timeouts, defaults of neglect, or buried settings.

### 7.6 Continuous accountability
Oversight is not a single checkbox. High-impact adaptations and assessment supports require renewed human attention at commitment points.

---

# PART VIII — Responsible Adaptation

### 8.1 Adapt resources, not children
Assistance may transform materials and pathways. It must not build durable learner deficit profiles as the engine of adaptation.

### 8.2 Teacher-declared context
Where context informs adaptation (language support, scaffold intensity, extension), it should derive from teacher-declared instructional intent and resource properties—not from covert profiling of children.

### 8.3 Shared intention preserved
Adapted variants must preserve the learning intention’s integrity. Adaptation that replaces purpose with busywork fails.

### 8.4 Dignified pathway language
Pathway and support language names supports and access—not deficits, diagnoses, or ranked identities.

### 8.5 Inclusive default craft
Responsible adaptation makes inclusive access ordinary: scaffolds, clearer language, alternate representations, and extensions—under teacher control.

### 8.6 Prohibition on diagnostic drift
Features that begin as “support” and drift into labeling, ability branding, or clinical sorting violate this Constitution even if renamed.

### 8.7 Measure of responsibility
Success is dignified access to worthy intentions—not volume of automated personalization.

---

# PART IX — Privacy and Trust

### 9.1 Data minimization
Collect and retain only what is necessary for declared educational purposes.

### 9.2 Purpose limitation
Data used for assistance may not be reused for surveillance, commercial profiling of children, or unrelated secondary aims.

### 9.3 Teacher control
Teachers retain control over pedagogical commitments and meaningful control over how their professional work is used within declared purposes.

### 9.4 Learner dignity in data
Learner-related information, where present, is held with heightened care. It must not become a ranking or stigma substrate.

### 9.5 Secure processing
Processing of entrusted data must meet the duty of care: protection against unauthorized access, misuse, and unjustified exposure.

### 9.6 Transparency of data use
Teachers must be able to understand, in plain language, what categories of information influence material recommendations.

### 9.7 Trust as operational duty
Trust is maintained by restraint, honesty about limits, recoverable failures, and refusal of prohibited behaviours—not by slogans.

# PART X — AI Quality Standards

Every AI capability must meet measurable quality standards before deployment and in ongoing stewardship.

| ID | Standard | Expectation |
|----|----------|-------------|
| **AIQ-01 Accuracy** | Educational content and transformations are factually and instructionally sound for the claimed purpose | Material error rates are reviewed; unsafe inaccuracy blocks release |
| **AIQ-02 Pedagogical relevance** | Outputs serve declared learning intentions and Pedagogical Framework principles | Irrelevant or off-intention counsel is rejected in review |
| **AIQ-03 Fairness** | Avoidable unjustified bias is identified and mitigated | Fairness review is mandatory for learner-affecting adaptations |
| **AIQ-04 Consistency** | Similar educational requests yield stable, coherent counsel | Wild contradiction without explanation fails reliability review |
| **AIQ-05 Reliability** | Behaviour is predictable under normal professional use; failures degrade safely | Unsafe silent failure is prohibited |
| **AIQ-06 Explainability** | Material recommendations carry educational rationale | Missing rationale blocks material counsel |
| **AIQ-07 Safety** | No prohibited behaviours; dignity and privacy preserved | Prohibited-behaviour presence fails AI Gate |
| **AIQ-08 Human oversight** | Outputs editable; approval required before educational use | Missing oversight controls fail AI Gate |
| **AIQ-09 Uncertainty honesty** | Limits and low confidence are communicable | Fabricated certainty fails review |
| **AIQ-10 Reversibility** | Teachers can reject, modify, and recover | Irreversible silent commitment fails Governance and AI Gates |

**Gate rule.** Failure of AIQ-06, AIQ-07, AIQ-08, or AIQ-10 fails the AI Gate. Failure of AIQ-02 fails the Pedagogical Gate when educational claims are made.

# PART XI — AI Review Framework

Before deploying any AI capability, stewards must answer:

### 11.1 Purpose and Dual Value
1. What educational problem does this assistance solve?  
2. How does teacher work become easier without coercion?  
3. How does learning access or quality improve?

### 11.2 Authority and oversight
4. Where does teacher approval occur before educational use?  
5. Can teachers edit, modify, reject, and recover?  
6. Can drafts silently become approved work?

### 11.3 Explainability and transparency
7. What educational rationale is available at decision time?  
8. Can a teacher restate why a recommendation was made?  
9. Are uncertainty and influence visible?

### 11.4 Prohibitions and dignity
10. Could this diagnose, label, rank, or stigmatize learners?  
11. Could this manipulate acceptance?  
12. Does adaptation act on resources rather than profiling children?

### 11.5 Privacy and quality
13. What data is necessary—and what is refused?  
14. Which AI Quality Standards are evidenced?  
15. What is the safe failure mode?

### 11.6 Necessity test
16. Would removing this AI capability improve trust, clarity, or educational integrity?

### 11.7 Decision rule
- If any prohibited behaviour is possible by design → reject or redesign.  
- If oversight or explainability is missing for material counsel → reject.  
- If Dual Value fails → return under Product Manifest discipline.  
- If (16) is yes → reject or redesign.  
- If Pedagogical Framework standards PQ-09 or PQ-11 are violated → reject.

Reviews record answers. Deployment without review is non-compliant.

---

# Constitutional Commitments (summary)

1. **Advice, not authority**  
2. **Explain before influence hardens**  
3. **Edit and approve before use**  
4. **Adapt resources, never brand children**  
5. **Minimize data; limit purpose**  
6. **Refuse when integrity is unclear**  
7. **Fail safely; reverse freely**  

---

# Closing

Assisting intelligence belongs in PRIMA only as a transparent colleague of the teacher’s craft—never as a hidden sovereign over learning.

Where assistance cannot be explained, overseen, and refused, it must not act.  
Where it would diminish dignity or seize judgment, it must not exist.

This is the AI Constitution of PRIMA.  
It binds every AI-powered feature henceforth.
